# Findings

## Medium Risk

### [M-01] One in-bound keeper call can roll a CYCLICAL asset's horizon a full cycle forward one block later

**Severity**

Medium

**Location**

`OctarineRFQ.sol#L141-L153`, `OctarineRFQ.sol#L112-L129`

**Description**

`SCHEDULE_KEEPER_ROLE` may update an asset's redemption schedule. Each update is bounded by `maxScheduleShiftSeconds` (default 7 days). The NatSpec of `setSchedule` gives the purpose of the bound:

Keepers are bounded by `maxScheduleShiftSeconds` so a compromised key cannot reprice the book by pushing an asset far out.

DESIGN.md §4 adds:

it checks both the live horizon and the cycle length, so a keeper cannot slip a long cycle in behind a small move today.

For a CYCLICAL asset, the horizon is a sawtooth. It counts down to the filing deadline (`nextRedemptionAt - redemptionLead`). Once `block.timestamp` reaches that deadline, it jumps up by `cycleSeconds`:

```solidity
uint256 cycle = s.cycleSeconds;
uint256 lead = s.redemptionLead;
uint256 deadline = s.nextRedemptionAt - lead;
if (block.timestamp >= deadline) {
    uint256 periods = ((block.timestamp - deadline) / cycle) + 1;
    deadline += periods * cycle;
}
uint256 remaining = deadline + lead - block.timestamp;
```

The bound only compares the old and new horizon at the current `block.timestamp`:

```solidity
uint256 before_ = _secondsFor(prev);
uint256 after_ = _secondsFor(s);
uint256 shift = before_ > after_ ? before_ - after_ : after_ - before_;
...
shift = Math.max(shift, shiftAmp);
if (shift > maxScheduleShiftSeconds) revert ScheduleShiftTooLarge(...);
```

A keeper can therefore move the filing deadline to `block.timestamp + 1`:

- At the moment of the call, the new horizon is tiny (`lead + 1`), so the measured shift is small and the call is accepted.
- One second later, the deadline has passed. The schedule rolls, and the horizon reads `cycleSeconds + lead`.

**Example** (from the PoC: 30-day cycle, `redemptionLead = 0`, `maxScheduleShiftSeconds = 7 days`):

| Time | Old schedule horizon | New schedule horizon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keeper call (payout 7 days out, keeper sets `nextRedemptionAt = now + 1`) | 7d | 1s (shift = 7d − 1s, accepted) |
| 12 seconds later | 7d − 12s | **30d − 11s** |

The same call can also stretch the cycle by the allowed 7 days. `shiftAmp` is 7 days, so `shift = max(7d − 1s, 7d) = 7d`, which is accepted. The horizon then reads **37d − 11s** instead of 7d − 12s: a 30-day jump from a single call that passes the bound.

The attack works whenever the live horizon is within `maxScheduleShiftSeconds + redemptionLead` of its minimum. On a monthly asset with the default bound, that is the last ~7 days before every filing deadline, about a quarter of the time.

**Root Cause**

The shift bound measures the horizon at a single instant (`block.timestamp`). A CYCLICAL horizon is discontinuous at the filing deadline. Moving the deadline right up to `block.timestamp` gives a small instantaneous shift. One block later, after the roll, the shift is close to a full cycle. The bound never looks at the horizon after the new deadline.

**Impact**

- **Every fill on the asset is mispriced** from the block after the update until the schedule is repaired. The discount is `makerBpsPerDay × horizon`, so the taker is charged for about 30 days of waiting instead of about 7.
- 10,000 RWA at 10 bps/day: the taker receives **9,700 USDC instead of ~9,930**. About **230 USDC (2.3% of notional)** moves from the taker to the maker.
- With the 7-day cycle stretch in the same call, the horizon goes from 7 days to 37 days: about **300 USDC (3%)** per 10,000 RWA.
- **The keeper cannot undo it in one call.** Restoring the previous schedule directly is a ~23-day shift and reverts with `ScheduleShiftTooLarge`. The keeper has to walk back in about four bounded calls, or the admin has to step in.
- **Takers' signed limits only partly protect them.**
- `takerMaxBpsPerDay` bounds the rate, not the horizon, so it does not help.
- `minReceivedAmount` limits the loss to the gap between the expected proceeds and each taker's floor.
- Open quotes (`taker == address(0)`) are protected only by a minimum the filler passes at call time.
- **It defeats the stated purpose of the bound.** The bound exists to stop a compromised keeper key from repricing the book. One call achieves 3–4× the allowed shift.

**Further single-call variants**

Re-testing the shift bound found two more single-call variants with the same root cause. They also show that refusing only deadlines moved earlier would not be enough.

**Root Cause (generalised)**

The bound compares two values per schedule:

- `shift`: the horizon at `block.timestamp`;
- `shiftAmp`: the longest horizon, which is `rollingSeconds` for ROLLING or `cycleSeconds` for CYCLICAL (the lead is fixed for keepers).

It never compares **where the next filing deadline is**, or **the shortest horizon** (just before each deadline). Two schedules can match on both values today and still diverge by close to a full cycle within days.

`shiftAmp` itself is consistent. It compares longest horizon against longest horizon, and the lead cancels because a keeper cannot change it (ROLLING forces it to 0). The gap is in what the bound does not look at.

**Case A: deadline pushed *later* just before it passes (the mirror of this issue; makers lose)**

- A 30-day CYCLICAL asset's roll is due in 1 hour. The keeper moves the payout 7 days later: `shift` = 7d − 1h, `shiftAmp` = 0, so the call is accepted.
- One hour later, the previous schedule would read 30d − 1h, but fills are priced at 7d − 2h.
- For 10,000 RWA at 10 bps/day, the maker pays **9,930.83 USDC instead of 9,700.42**. About **230 USDC (2.3%) moves from maker to taker** on every fill for the next ~7 days.
- A maker whose `maxMakerAmount` is sized to its own 30-day view is protected: the fill reverts with `MakerAmountTooHigh` (shown in the PoC). Makers who quote looser caps are not protected.

**Case B: mode switch (`shiftAmp` compares longest horizons, never shortest)**

- **ROLLING 30d → CYCLICAL 30d** with the payout 23 days out: `shift` 7d, `shiftAmp` 0, accepted. Twenty-three days later the horizon is 1 hour, while the asset is still T+30. The maker overpays by about **300 USDC per 10,000 RWA**.
- **CYCLICAL 30d → ROLLING 23d** with the payout 16 days out: `shift` 7d, `shiftAmp` 7d, accepted. An hour before the real payout, the taker is still charged 23 days of discount and receives about **230 USDC per 10,000 RWA** too little.
- The keeper runbook (README, "Schedule keeper (CYCLICAL assets)") never involves a mode change.

**Recommendation**

1. Make `mode` admin-only once an asset is configured. This follows the code's own reasoning for `redemptionLead` ("decides which payout a fill is priced to").
2. Treat the next filing deadline the same in both directions: if a keeper update changes it, and either the old or the new deadline is within `maxScheduleShiftSeconds` of now, only the admin may make the change.

```solidity
// after the existing lead check
if (!isAdmin && prev.configured && s.mode != prev.mode) revert Unauthorized();

// inside the existing keeper-bound block, after the shift check
if (s.mode == ScheduleMode.CYCLICAL) {
    uint256 pd = _nextDeadline(prev);
    uint256 nd = _nextDeadline(s);
    if (pd != nd && Math.min(pd, nd) < block.timestamp + maxScheduleShiftSeconds) {
        revert ScheduleShiftTooLarge(before_, after_, maxScheduleShiftSeconds);
    }
}

function _nextDeadline(RedemptionSchedule memory s) internal view returns (uint256 deadline) {
    deadline = s.nextRedemptionAt - s.redemptionLead;
    if (block.timestamp >= deadline) {
        deadline += (((block.timestamp - deadline) / s.cycleSeconds) + 1) * s.cycleSeconds;
    }
}
```

Residual risk: a deadline move outside the window still opens a divergence window later, as any legitimate holiday move does. It is announced at least 7 days ahead through `ScheduleUpdated`.

Not included: changing `cycleSeconds` alone on an old anchor also moves the live payout, but the README runbook already documents that (rule 1).

```solidity
        vm.prank(admin);
        rfq.setSchedule(address(rwa), _cyclical(uint64(T0 + 7 days), 30, 100 * BPD));

        vm.prank(keeper);
        vm.expectRevert(

        // One keeper call: anchor one second out. Live shift = 7d - 1s <= 7d, cycle unchanged: accepted.
        vm.prank(keeper);
        rfq.setSchedule(address(rwa), _cyclical(uint64(T0 + 1), 30, 100 * BPD));
        assertEq(rfq.secondsToRedemption(address(rwa)), 1);

        // One block later the anchor has rolled: the horizon is a full cycle.
        vm.warp(T0 + 12);
        _refreshPrice();
        assertEq(rfq.secondsToRedemption(address(rwa)), 30 days - 11);

        // Price impact on a 10,000 RWA fill at 10.00 bps/day.
        IOctarineRFQ.RFQOrder memory order = _order(10 * BPD, 10_000e18, 0);
        (uint256 makerAmt,,) = rfq.quoteRFQOrder(order, 10_000e18);
        // Previous schedule at the same instant: 7d - 12s horizon -> ~9,930 USDC.
        uint256 oldAmt = uint256(10_000e6) * (8.64e10 - uint256(10 * BPD) * (7 days - 12)) / 8.64e10;
        emit log_named_uint("maker pays (new schedule)", makerAmt);
        emit log_named_uint("maker pays (old schedule)", oldAmt);
        assertApproxEqAbs(oldAmt - makerAmt, 230e6, 1e6); // ~230 USDC per 10k (2.3%) moved to the maker
    }

    /// The same single call can also stretch the cycle by the allowed 7 days: a 30-day jump.
    function test_earlyRollPlusCycleStretch() public {
        vm.prank(admin);
        rfq.setSchedule(address(rwa), _cyclical(uint64(T0 + 7 days), 30, 100 * BPD));
        vm.prank(keeper);
        rfq.setSchedule(address(rwa), _cyclical(uint64(T0 + 1), 37, 100 * BPD)); // shift = max(7d-1, 7d) = 7d
        vm.warp(T0 + 12);
        assertEq(rfq.secondsToRedemption(address(rwa)), 37 days - 11); // previous schedule: 7d - 12s
    }
}
```

**Team Response**

Fixed.

The schedule keeper is a trusted role, `maxScheduleShiftSeconds` is defence in depth (README Assumption 2), and the README already accepts that batched calls exceed it. Exposure is bounded by both parties' signed limits: makers cap `maxMakerAmount` from their own NAV and horizon, as the Case A proof of concept shows with the cap reverting the fill, and named takers sign a `minReceivedAmount` floor. Fixed as recommended: once an asset is configured, only the admin can change its `mode`, and a keeper cannot move the next filing deadline when the current or the proposed deadline is less than `maxScheduleShiftSeconds` away.

## Low Risk

### [L-01] Maker cancellation is keyed on the taker-chosen salt

**Severity**

Low

**Location**

`OctarineRFQ.sol#L304-L308`, `OctarineRFQ.sol#L419`

**Description**

An `RFQOrder` is the taker's signed `Request` plus three maker fields: `makerBpsPerDay`, `maxMakerAmount` and `maker`. Its only nonce is `salt`, which is a **request** field. The taker picks it and signs it before any maker bids. The maker has no nonce of its own.

Cancellation works per `(account, salt)` and is checked for the maker against the request's salt:

```solidity
function cancelSalt(address account, uint256 salt) external {
    if (account != msg.sender && !orderSigners[account][msg.sender]) revert Unauthorized();
    saltCancelled[account][salt] = true;
    ...
}

// _validateCommonTerms
if (saltCancelled[maker][r.salt]) revert SaltIsCancelled(maker, r.salt);
```

When a maker calls `cancelSalt(maker, s)` to retract one bid, it actually:

1. Kills **every** bid it has signed on **any** request from **any** taker that uses salt `s`.
2. Permanently blocks itself from filling **any future request** that uses salt `s`.
3. Cannot retract one bid and keep a replacement. A re-quote on the same request has the same salt, so cancelling the stale bid also kills the new one. Not cancelling leaves the stale bid fillable.

README Assumption 3 documents that `cancelSalt` voids "every unfilled order made on its behalf under a salt". What it does not say is that, for a maker, the salt is chosen by the taker. As a result, the maker's cancel reaches other takers' requests, and the maker cannot replace one bid with another.

**Root Cause**

The maker's signature (`RFQ_ORDER_TYPEHASH`) does not cover any maker-chosen nonce or salt. Maker cancellation reuses the taker's salt as its key. The key is shared across every request that uses the same salt value, rather than being specific to one order.

**Impact**

- **Makers cannot cancel and replace bids.** Re-quoting is the normal RFQ workflow when funding costs or market conditions change. The stale bid stays fillable, and the taker or anyone holding both signatures will take the better one.
- In the PoC, the maker re-quotes from 10 to 20 bps/day on a 30-day asset. The stale bid is filled: the maker pays **970 USDC instead of the 940 it re-quoted** per 1,000 RWA, **30 USDC (3% of notional)** more than its current price.
- The only alternative is to cancel the salt, which loses the trade and blocks the maker from that request for good.
- **Collateral cancellation across unrelated takers.** If takers' salts are not random, for example sequential (0, 1, 2, …) or timestamps, different takers' requests share salts. Cancelling one bid then also kills the maker's live bids for other takers. With random 256-bit salts, collisions are negligible.
- **Permanent lockout.** Every cancel permanently removes a salt value from the set of requests the maker can serve. This matters only when takers reuse salt values.
- **Combined with L-02**, the maker has no way to bound how long a bid lives other than a salt-burning cancel or revoking a delegated signer key, which suspends its whole book.

**Recommendation**

Give the maker its own cancellation key:

- Add a maker-chosen `makerSalt` (or `makerNonce`) to `RFQOrder` and `RFQ_ORDER_TYPEHASH`, and check `saltCancelled[maker][order.makerSalt]` instead of the request salt for the maker side; **or**
- Add `cancelOrder(bytes32 orderHash)` with an `orderCancelled[orderHash]` mapping checked in `fillRFQOrder`.

Optionally add a per-maker `minNonce` (bulk cancel by epoch), so a maker can retract its whole book without revoking signer keys.

```solidity
// SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT
pragma solidity ^0.8.24;

import {OctarineRFQBase} from "../unit/OctarineRFQBase.sol";
import {IOctarineRFQ} from "../../src/octarine-rfq/interfaces/IOctarineRFQ.sol";

contract M02_MakerSaltCancel is OctarineRFQBase {
    uint256 internal taker2Pk = 0x7A4E2;
    address internal taker2;

    function setUp() public override {
        super.setUp();
        taker2 = vm.addr(taker2Pk);
        rwa.mint(taker2, 1_000_000e18);
        vm.prank(taker2);
        rwa.approve(address(rfq), type(uint256).max);
    }

    function _named(address t, uint256 salt, uint128 bps) internal view returns (IOctarineRFQ.RFQOrder memory o) {
        o = _order(bps, 1000e18, 0);
        o.taker = t;
        o.salt = salt;
        o.takerMaxBpsPerDay = 50 * BPD;
        o.minReceivedAmount = 900e6;
    }

    /// The maker cannot retract a stale bid while keeping its replacement: both share the
    /// taker's salt, so the only cancel kills both, and the stale one stays fillable otherwise.
    function test_makerCannotCancelAndReplaceABid() public {
        IOctarineRFQ.RFQOrder memory v1 = _named(taker, 42, 10 * BPD); // stale bid
        IOctarineRFQ.RFQOrder memory v2 = _named(taker, 42, 20 * BPD); // maker's replacement
        bytes memory s1 = _signOrder(makerPk, v1);
        bytes memory s2 = _signOrder(makerPk, v2);
        bytes memory reqSig = _signRequest(takerPk, v1); // same request for both
        assertEq(keccak256(reqSig), keccak256(_signRequest(takerPk, v2)));

        // Retracting v1 is only possible via cancelSalt(maker, 42) -> v2 dies too.
        uint256 snap = vm.snapshotState();
        vm.prank(maker);
        rfq.cancelSalt(maker, 42);
        vm.expectRevert(abi.encodeWithSelector(IOctarineRFQ.SaltIsCancelled.selector, maker, uint256(42)));
        vm.prank(outsider);
        rfq.fillRFQOrder(v2, s2, reqSig, 1000e18);
        vm.revertToState(snap);

        // Without the cancel, the taker (or anyone holding both sigs) picks the stale, better bid.
        vm.prank(outsider);
        rfq.fillRFQOrder(v1, s1, reqSig, 1000e18);
        assertEq(usdc.balanceOf(taker), 970e6); // 10 bps/day, not the maker's re-quoted 20 (940)
    }

    /// Cancelling one bid also voids every other bid by the same maker on any request that
    /// reuses the salt value, including requests signed after the cancel.
    function test_makerCancelVoidsUnrelatedRequestsSharingTheSalt() public {
        IOctarineRFQ.RFQOrder memory onB = _named(taker2, 7, 10 * BPD); // unrelated taker, same salt
        bytes memory sB = _signOrder(makerPk, onB);
        bytes memory reqB = _signRequest(taker2Pk, onB);

        // Maker only means to retract its bid on taker A's request (salt 7).
        vm.prank(maker);
        rfq.cancelSalt(maker, 7);

        vm.prank(outsider);
        vm.expectRevert(abi.encodeWithSelector(IOctarineRFQ.SaltIsCancelled.selector, maker, uint256(7)));
        rfq.fillRFQOrder(onB, sB, reqB, 1000e18);

        // And forever: a request taker B signs next month with salt 7 can never be filled by this maker.
        vm.warp(T0 + 30 days);
        _refreshPrice();
        IOctarineRFQ.RFQOrder memory later = _named(taker2, 7, 10 * BPD);
        later.expiry = uint64(block.timestamp + 1 hours);
        bytes memory sL = _signOrder(makerPk, later);
        bytes memory reqL = _signRequest(taker2Pk, later);
        vm.prank(outsider);
        vm.expectRevert(abi.encodeWithSelector(IOctarineRFQ.SaltIsCancelled.selector, maker, uint256(7)));
        rfq.fillRFQOrder(later, sL, reqL, 1000e18);
    }
}
```

**Team Response**

Fixed.

Rated Low. The cross-taker and lockout effects need takers to reuse salt values, and Octarine's frontend and backend generate random 256-bit salts. A signed bid is a firm quote that the taker can execute as soon as it is received, so cancel-and-replace is a race in any RFQ design. A bid lives no longer than the request's expiry, and only one bid per request can fill. Fixed: a maker can now void a single bid with `cancelOrder` without touching the request's salt.

### [L-02] A maker cannot give its bid a shorter expiry than the taker's request

**Severity**

Low

**Location**

`OctarineRFQ.sol#L414`

**Description**

`expiry` is a `Request` field. `fillRFQOrder` rebuilds the request from the order's fields and verifies the taker's signature over it. The maker's signed expiry must therefore equal the taker's. If a maker signs a shorter expiry, the rebuilt request no longer matches the taker's signature and the fill reverts with `InvalidSignature`.

The NatSpec nonetheless tells both sides to "keep signing short windows", and README Assumption 3 names "a **short** `expiry`" as one of the maker's primary bounds. In practice the maker's only control is to refuse to bid on requests whose window is too long.

**Root Cause**

`RFQOrder` has no maker-side expiry. The only expiry checked (`r.expiry <= block.timestamp`) is the taker's.

**Impact**

- A maker's bid stays valid for the taker's entire window, which can be days or weeks. During that time the maker must keep allowance and balance in place, and its funding cost or risk appetite may change. The taker effectively holds a free option on the maker.
- The rate model tracks the oracle price, but not the maker's changing cost of capital.
- Because of L-01, the maker cannot cleanly retract the bid either.

In the PoC, a maker signs a 5-minute bid against a 30-day request. The 5-minute version reverts. The 30-day version is filled on day 29.

**Recommendation**

Add `makerExpiry` to `RFQOrder` and `RFQ_ORDER_TYPEHASH`, and check it in `fillRFQOrder`:

```solidity
if (order.makerExpiry <= block.timestamp) revert OrderExpired(order.makerExpiry);
```

### [L-03] One payee whose transfer reverts blocks distribution of that token for every payee for at least 48h

**Severity**

Low

**Location**

`FeeSplitter.sol#L49-L62`

**Description**

`distribute(token)` pays every payee in one loop with `safeTransfer`. If any single transfer reverts, the whole call reverts. For example:

- the payee is blacklisted on USDC or USDT;
- the token is a transfer-restricted RWA and the payee is not on its allowlist.

There is no skip, pull-payment or sweep path.

**Root Cause**

Push-based payout of all shares in one transaction. The only way to remove a payee is a payee-table change, which is behind the 48-hour `TIMELOCK`.

**Impact**

- In the PoC, a payee with a 10% share is blacklisted, which freezes the other 90% of that token's fees for at least 48 hours.
- The table change that fixes it is global, so the blocked payee also loses its share of **every other token**, including tokens it can still receive.

**Recommendation**

Use pull payments. Record each payee's share per token on `distribute`, and let payees `claim(token)` themselves. Alternatively, wrap each transfer in a try/catch and credit failed transfers to a claimable balance.

### [L-05] Colluding Signers will bypass the maxChangeBps limit to artificially inflate price, draining Makers

**Severity**

Low

**Location**

`OctarineThirdPartySetterOracle.sol#L89-L104`

**Summary**

The OctarineThirdPartySetterOracle enforces a maxChangeBps limit per signer to prevent massive price swings. However, because this limit is applied per-signer, multiple colluding signers can push back-to-back updates in the same block. This allows them to cumulatively bypass the maximum allowable change and artificially manipulate the price.

**Root Cause**

The choice to enforce maxChangeBps strictly per-signer (_deviates(prev, newPrice, maxChangeBps)) rather than globally across all signers in a given timeframe is a mistake because it allows N colluding signers to move the price by N × maxChangeBps simultaneously.

**Internal Pre-conditions**

- Two or more signers need to be active in the OctarineThirdPartySetterOracle.

- The signers need to be malicious or colluding.

- A Maker needs to submit an RFQ order without a tight maxMakerAmount (e.g., leaving it at type(uint128).max).

**External Pre-conditions**

None

**Attack Path**

- Maker signs an RFQ order expecting the current price ($100) to remain stable, leaving maxMakerAmount wide open.

- Signer 1 calls pushPrice(110e18) (a 10% increase).

- Signer 2 calls pushPrice(121e18) in the same block (another 10% increase).

- The Attacker (acting as the Taker) immediately calls fillRFQOrder, exploiting the inflated $121 price to drain the Maker.

**Impact**

The Maker suffers a loss equal to the artificial price delta

**Recommendation**

Implement a global rate limit (e.g., a rolling window) across all signers, rather than per-signer. Track lastGlobalUpdate and lastGlobalPrice to ensure the cumulative price change across all signers does not exceed maxChangeBps within a specific timeframe.

### [I-01] OctarineRFQ setSchedule permits unfillable maxBpsPerDay exceeding discount denominator during lead window

**Severity**

Informational

**Location**

`OctarineRFQ.sol#L163-L186`

**Summary**

In `OctarineRFQ`, cyclical asset pricing calculates a price discount as a function of the order's discount rate (`makerBpsPerDay`) multiplied by the remaining seconds to redemption (`horizonSeconds`). The maximum permissible rate is capped by `schedule.maxBpsPerDay`. During the filing cutoff lead window before a redemption payout (`block.timestamp >= deadline`), the effective horizon rolls over to include the subsequent cycle (`cycleSeconds + redemptionLead`). Because `_validateSchedule` does not enforce that the maximum discount under the peak horizon remains below `DISCOUNT_DENOMINATOR`, a keeper can configure a valid schedule where orders quoted at rates within `maxBpsPerDay` succeed during the normal cycle but revert unconditionally with `DiscountExceedsPrincipal` during the lead window. Attacker privilege: `SCHEDULE_KEEPER_ROLE` (or `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`).

**Root Cause**

In `src/octarine-rfq/OctarineRFQ.sol:163-186`, schedule validation checks individual parameter boundaries but does not validate the joint product of rate and maximum horizon:

```solidity
    function _validateSchedule(RedemptionSchedule calldata s) internal view {
        if (s.maxBpsPerDay == 0) revert InvalidSchedule();

        if (s.mode == ScheduleMode.ROLLING) {
            // One second is the floor: a zero horizon would price every fill at no spread at all.
            if (s.rollingSeconds < MIN_HORIZON_SECONDS || s.rollingSeconds > MAX_REDEMPTION_SECONDS) {
                revert InvalidSchedule();
            }
            if (s.redemptionLead != 0) revert InvalidSchedule();
        } else {
            // Just past a deadline the horizon is `cycleSeconds + redemptionLead`, the longest it gets.
            if (
                s.cycleSeconds < MIN_HORIZON_SECONDS
                    || uint256(s.cycleSeconds) + s.redemptionLead > MAX_REDEMPTION_SECONDS
            ) {
                revert InvalidSchedule();
            }
            // Also refuses a zero anchor; a deadline before the epoch would underflow in pricing.
            if (s.nextRedemptionAt <= s.redemptionLead) revert InvalidSchedule();
            if (s.redemptionLead >= s.cycleSeconds) revert InvalidSchedule();
            // Until the first anchor passes, the horizon is not yet capped by `cycleSeconds`.
            if (s.nextRedemptionAt > block.timestamp + MAX_REDEMPTION_SECONDS) revert InvalidSchedule();
        }
    }
```


**Attack Path**

| # | Actor | Call | Precondition | State change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Keeper | `setSchedule(asset, s)` | `SCHEDULE_KEEPER_ROLE` | `cycleSeconds = 30 days`, `redemptionLead = 2 days`, `maxBpsPerDay = 35_000` (3.5%/day) |
| 2 | Maker & Taker | RFQ quote & fill during mid-cycle | `block.timestamp < deadline` (e.g. Day 10) | Horizon = 20 days. `discount = 60_480_000_000 < DISCOUNT_DENOMINATOR`. Trade succeeds. |
| 3 | Market | Clock reaches filing deadline | `block.timestamp == deadline` (Day 28) | Horizon jumps to 30 + 2 = 32 days. |
| 4 | Maker & Taker | Attempt quote / fill at 3.5%/day | Rate ≤ `maxBpsPerDay` | `discount = 96_768_000_000 >= 86_400_000_000`. Reverts `DiscountExceedsPrincipal`. |

Legitimate market participants attempting to trade high-yield or distressed short-cycle paper cannot settle signed orders during the cutoff window, resulting in unexpected transaction failure.

**Worked example**

1. The keeper sets `cycleSeconds = 30 days` (2,592,000 s) and `redemptionLead = 2 days` (172,800 s), so the longest horizon is 2,764,800 s (32 days).
2. The keeper sets `maxBpsPerDay = 35,000` (350 bps per day, a 3.5% daily discount rate).
3. Before the deadline (day 10) the horizon is 20 days (1,728,000 s): 35,000 × 1,728,000 = 60,480,000,000 < 86,400,000,000, so the fill succeeds.
4. At the deadline (day 28) the horizon is 32 days (2,764,800 s): 35,000 × 2,764,800 = 96,768,000,000 ≥ 86,400,000,000, so the fill reverts.

### [I-02] Pending signers never expire, and a revoke can be front-run while still emitting `SignerRevoked`

**Severity**

Informational

**Location**

`OctarineThirdPartySetterOracle.sol#L120-L145`

**Description**

Adding a signer is a two-step, timelocked process: `submitSigner` and then `acceptSigner`.

- `acceptSigner` is callable by **anyone** once the timelock has passed.
- The submission **never expires**.
- `revokePendingSigner` deletes `signerSubmittedAt[signer]` and emits `SignerRevoked` unconditionally. It does not check whether anything was pending, and it does not touch `isSigner`.

**Root Cause**

- `acceptSigner` is permissionless.
- Submissions have no acceptance window.
- `revokePendingSigner` neither reverts nor clears `isSigner` when the signer was already accepted.

**Impact**

- **Front-run revoke.** The owner decides to cancel a pending signer after its timelock has passed. The candidate, or anyone, front-runs with `acceptSigner`. The owner's `revokePendingSigner` then succeeds and emits `SignerRevoked`, but the candidate stays an active signer and can push prices. Monitoring and the owner both believe the signer was revoked.
- **Stale submissions.** A submission can be accepted a year later, or after an ownership transfer, inheriting a decision the new owner never made.
- An unwanted signer can move the price by up to `maxChangeBps` (up to 25%) per `minUpdateInterval`. The RFQ prices fills from that price.

**Recommendation**

- Restrict `acceptSigner` to the owner, or to the signer itself.
- Add an acceptance window: `validAt <= block.timestamp <= validAt + acceptWindow`.
- In `revokePendingSigner`, revert with `NoPendingSigner()` if nothing is pending, so a lost race is visible instead of silent.
- Optionally clear pending submissions on ownership transfer.

### [I-03] A malformed, codeless or overflowing direct feed blocks the reference-asset fallback

**Severity**

Informational

**Location**

`OctarineRFQ.sol#L210-L219`

**Description**

`priceOf` is designed to fall back to triangulating through `referenceAsset` when the direct feed is "absent, reverting, zero or stale". Three cases revert in the caller instead of returning 0:

- the feed returns data shorter than two words (an ABI decode failure is not caught by `try/catch`);
- the registered address has no code;
- the feed reports an `updatedAt` large enough that `updatedAt + oracleMaxStaleness` overflows (a panic in the caller).

**Root Cause**

`try/catch` only catches reverts inside the callee. The decode and the staleness addition happen in the caller.

**Impact**

A bad direct-feed registration blocks the pair entirely even though a healthy fallback route exists. The in-scope oracles never return such data, so this requires a misconfigured or foreign feed.

**Recommendation**

- Read the feed with a low-level `staticcall` and a return-length check, as `OctarineOracleBase._read` does.
- Compare with `updatedAt > block.timestamp || block.timestamp - updatedAt > staleness` instead of adding.
- Require `oracle.code.length > 0` in `registerOracle`.
