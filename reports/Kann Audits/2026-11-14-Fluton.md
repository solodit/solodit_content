# Findings

## Informational

### [I-01] Deposit screening ledger is unbounded and signer rotation orphans all outstanding screenings

**Severity**

Informational

**Location**

`PrivacyPool.sol`

**Location:** PrivacyPool.sol lines 703 to 717 (`_spendScreening`, with the `screeningUsed` mapping at line 199), 401 to 409 (`setScreeningSigner`, `_setScreeningSigner`), 308 to 336 (`initialize`).

**Description.** The deposit screening mechanism added in this change keeps two pieces of state that grow or invalidate without any cleanup path:

1. The `screeningUsed` mapping records every nonce ever spent and is never pruned. Since a screening is single use, and the backend issues a fresh 32 byte nonce for every deposit request, every screened deposit permanently adds one mapping entry (20,000 gas of storage) that is never read again after the deposit completes. Over the life of the pool this ledger only grows.
2. `setScreeningSigner` replaces the signing key with no coordination with the backend. Every screening the old signer issued dies at that moment (its signature no longer recovers to the stored signer), including ones already issued, paid for and not yet consumed by a pending deposit. Users who obtained a screening and have not yet submitted their deposit must request a new one from the backend, which must in turn be re-keyed to the new signer.

Additionally, an in-place upgrade from the previous implementation starts with `screeningSigner` unset, so every deposit reverts with `ScreeningSignerUnset` until the owner calls `setScreeningSigner`. This is intentional (screening cannot be silently off) but the deploy runbook should call it out, and the same applies on every future upgrade that touches this contract.

None of these is exploitable: the ledger cannot be manipulated to re-arm a spent nonce, rotating the signer cannot be triggered by anyone but the owner, and no funds are at risk at any point. The finding is recorded so the operational costs are known and a future revision can consider bounded cleanup (for example, pruning entries older than `MAX_SCREENING_TTL`, which is safe because an expired screening can never be spent).

**Impact.** No fund loss and no bypass. Permanent storage growth (one slot per screened deposit), one-time deposit outage across a signer rotation or upgrade until the owner sets the signer, and re-issuance overhead for users holding unconsumed screenings at that moment.

**Recommendation.**

- Document the permanent growth of `screeningUsed` and the post-upgrade `setScreeningSigner` step in the deployment runbook.
- Optionally prune ledger entries once `block.timestamp > expiresAt`: an expired screening can never be spent, so the entry is dead weight. Note the backend must never re-issue a nonce it issued before, which it already guarantees with 32 random bytes.
- Coordinate signer rotation with the screening API so outstanding screenings are re-issued under the new key at rotation time.

**Status**

Acknowledged.

### [I-02] Deposit screenings are signed for the depositor only, and the nonce is not bound to the deposit parameters

**Severity**

Informational

**Location**

`PrivacyPool.sol`

**Location:** PrivacyPool.sol lines 703 to 717 (`_spendScreening`), 56 to 57 (`DEPOSIT_SCREENING_TYPEHASH`), 161 to 165 (`DepositScreening` struct).

**Description.** The deposit screening message is `DepositScreening(address depositor, bytes32 nonce, uint64 expiresAt)` signed by `screeningSigner`. The struct deliberately omits everything else about the deposit: the asset, the amount, the format and the deposit route (one-step TOKEN deposit vs eTOKEN `requestDeposit`). A screening is also not consumed by a specific deposit, it only marks the depositor as screened once.

This is a deliberate design (the NatSpec describes the screening as a check of the depositor, and the deposit parameters are separately bound into the join-split proof through `extDataHash`), but it has two consequences worth recording:

1. One screening authorizes exactly one deposit of any size, in any listed asset, through either route. A user who obtained a screening for a 1 unit deposit can spend it on a 1,000,000 unit deposit instead. The backend's screening decision (risk scoring of the depositor) does not scale with the amount being shielded, so the screened amount is bounded only by the depositor's own balance, not by anything the screener saw.
2. The nonce is not tied to the deposit at all, so a user who obtained several screenings while cleared by screening can hold them and submit them later, in any order, at any time before their individual `expiresAt`. The `expiresAt` upper bound (one hour) caps this hold time, which is what keeps the window tight.

Since the screening's purpose is depositor risk screening rather than amount authorization, and the deposit parameters are bound into the proof, this does not break any invariant of the pool. It is recorded so it is a known property: the screener vouches for who is depositing, never for what or how much.

**Impact.** None on pool solvency or user funds. The screener's risk decision applies to the depositor's identity only; deposit size and asset remain unrestricted within the pool's own limits.

**Recommendation.** None required for correctness. If the backend ever wants to bound screened amounts, include the asset and amount (or a hash of the deposit parameters) in the `DepositScreening` struct and mirror them in the route's request body. The current one-hour expiry is the effective bound on how long a screening can be held.

**Status**

Acknowledged.
